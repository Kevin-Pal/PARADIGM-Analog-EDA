The problem

Optimization starts one decision too late.

Analog IC design has two coupled choices: which topology can meet the brief, and how to size its transistors. Most automation tackles only the second.

A

Choose the topology

A structural decision grounded in experience. Choose poorly, and no amount of downstream tuning makes the search efficient.

B

Size the devices

A continuous, multi-objective optimization problem. Existing black-box methods are useful here—but inherit the topology decision.

PARADIGM makes topology selection part of the automated design loop.

How it works

Build knowledge once. Apply it repeatedly.

Two stages separate the expensive offline exploration from fast, database-guided design-time decisions.

01

DSE

Design-space exploration

TuRBO first pushes rapidly toward the performance boundary using weighted scalar objectives. NSGA-II then expands laterally using Pareto dominance, trading a narrow fast search for broad front coverage.

02

TP

Topology prediction

Performance targets become features and topology names become classes. Training on Pareto-optimal samples reflects a practical bias: select the topology that meets the brief without costly overdesign.

PT

Parameter tuning

P2C Net compresses database knowledge into a simulation-free initial point. TuRBO-1 then refines that prediction locally instead of spending its initialization budget rediscovering the database.
